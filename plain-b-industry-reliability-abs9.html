In perovskite solar cells, the interfaces between the perovskite and charge-transporting layers contain high concentrations of defects (about 100 times that within the perovskite layer), specifically, deep-level defects, which substantially reduce the power conversion efficiency of the devices1-3. Recent efforts to reduce these interfacial defects have focused mainly on surface passivation4-6. However, passivating the perovskite surface that interfaces with the electron-transporting layer is difficult, because the surface-treatment agents on the electron-transporting layer may dissolve while coating the perovskite thin film. Alternatively, interfacial defects may not be a concern if a coherent interface could be formed between the electron-transporting and perovskite layers. Here we report the formation of an interlayer between a SnO2 electron-transporting layer and a halide perovskite light-absorbing layer, achieved by coupling Cl-bonded SnO2 with a Cl-containing perovskite precursor. This interlayer has atomically coherent features, which enhance charge extraction and transport from the perovskite layer, and fewer interfacial defects. The existence of such a coherent interlayer allowed us to fabricate perovskite solar cells with a power conversion efficiency of 25.8 per cent (certified 25.5 per cent)under standard illumination. Furthermore, unencapsulated devices maintained about 90 per cent of their initial efficiency even after continuous light exposure for 500 hours. “One-key-reset” recycling of whole perovskite solar cell

2021-06-23 · Matter · DOI 10.1016/j.matt.2021.05.023 · [F3*]

Kai Wang, Tao Ye, Xu Huang, Yuchen Hou, Jungjin Yoon, Dong Yang, Xiaowen Hu, Xiaofang Jiang, Congcong Wu, Guofu Zhou, Shashank Priya

Summary Sustainably recycling photovoltaic (PV) products at the end of their life cycle is essential for reducing environmental impact and saving costs on raw materials. Halide perovskite PVs currently suffer from a short lifespan, and recycling and remaking could equivalently extend their life cycle. Here, we report an effective and rapid “one-key reset” recycling paradigm to collect all the functional layer components from a used PV device at a high material extraction efficiency. A remade device incorporating all the layers, including recycled indium tin oxide/tin oxide substrate, perovskite, spiro-OMeTAD, and gold, exhibits an average power conversion efficiency of over 20%. This finding that the recycled device exhibits identical performance compared with the original, while providing enormous savings in cost, time, and energy, could lead to a recycling route that will dramatically lower the levelized cost of energy of perovskite PVs and enhance their competitiveness in future energy markets.

Summary Although great progress is being made toward improving the power conversion efficiency (PCE) and the operational stability of perovskite solar cells (PSCs), little attention is being paid to their mechanical reliability, which is particularly important for flexible PSCs (f-PCSs). Here, we have grown low-dimensional (LD) metal-halide perovskite (MHP) thin capping layer over the 3D MHP light-absorber thin film in f-PSCs in situ to improve their mechanical reliability. This resulted in f-PSCs with unprecedented, simultaneous improvements in PCE (21.0%), operational stability (T90, retention of 90% of the initial PCE, >800 h), and bending durability (T80 = 20,000 tension-only bending cycles). These synergistic virtuous effects are attributed to the LD MHP capping layer enhancing photocarriers extraction, providing protection against the environment, and “sealing” surface flaws on the 3D MHP thin film. The latter reduces the propensity for cracking in bending, which results in improved environmental stability and bending durability.

Ink Engineering for Blade Coating FA-Dominated Perovskites in Ambient Air for Efficient Solar Cells and Modules

2021-04-16 · ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · DOI 10.1021/acsami.1c00900 · [D4* E1]

Hengyi Li, Tongle Bu, Jing Li, Zhipeng Lin, Junye Pan, Qianhui Li, Xiao-Li Zhang, Zhiliang Ku, Yi-Bing Cheng, Fuzhi Huang

To accelerate the commercial application of organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells (PSCs), it is necessary to develop simple and low-cost methods to prepare pinhole-free large-area perovskite films with high quality. A one-step blade coating method is regarded as a scalable technique. It is demonstrated that with the addition of N,N'-dimethylpropyleneurea (DMPU) in an FA-dominated perovskite precursor, a large-area high-quality perovskite film can be obtained by blade coating, achieving improved photovoltaic performance, thermal stability, and storage stability. It is found that the strong interaction between DMPU and Pb2+ ions is beneficial to delay the nucleation crystallization process, increase the size of crystal grains, and improve the crystallinity of the perovskite film. Planar n-i-p solar cells introducing DMPU exhibit power conversion efficiencies of 20.20% for 0.16 cm2 devices and 17.71% for 5 × 5 cm2 modules with an aperture area of 10 cm2. In addition, the devices without encapsulation placed at 50 °C for 500 h and with a relative humidity of 20 ± 5% for 1000 h still maintain efficiencies above 80 and 90%, respectively, showing outstanding stability.

Volatile solution: the way toward scalable fabrication of perovskite solar cells?

2021-03-07 · Matter · REVIEW · DOI 10.1016/j.matt.2020.12.025 · [D4*]

Congcong Wu, Kai Wang, Jing Li, Zihui Liang, Jin Li, Wenlu Li, Li Zhao, Bo Chi, Shimin Wang

In the past decade, perovskite photovoltaics have achieved impressive progress in both efficiency and stability, bringing new insights and excitement in industrial sectors. Transitioning this technology from the laboratory to the industrial level first needs to overcome the crucial barrier of scalable fabrication. Although various synthetic routes have been developed for obtaining high-quality perovskite layers, there remains a gap between small-scale fabrication and industrial-level manufacturing, as the incumbent processing usually requires complex and energy-consuming treatments to remove the non-volatile solvents from those conventional perovskite inks. In this perspective, we conceptualize the volatile solution as an alternative ink system serving as a roadmap toward reliable manufacturing in the future. With this comprehensive minireview on the ongoing research and a discussion of its hypothetical potential/challenge, we hope to bring new inspiration and to catalyze the transition.

Metal-Chelating Molecule-Enabled Multidentate Molecular Bridge Engineering of the Buried Interface for High-Performance Fully Printed Carbon-Based Perovskite Solar Modules

2026-11-23 · Advanced Materials · DOI 10.1002/adma.75783 · [D4* D5 F1 F2]

Chunyu Lv, Changqing Lin, Kai Zhang, Gaofeng Li, Azhu Wang, Xiaozhen Wei, Weibiao Zhong, Qifeng Lin, Xing Tang, Xianzhen Huang, Zhihao Lei, Huicong Liu, Weiping Li, Haining Chen, Shihe Yang

ABSTRACT Carbon-based perovskite solar modules (C-PSMs) provide a scalable and metal-electrode-free platform for stable perovskite photovoltaics, yet their efficiencies remain constrained by defective and mechanically fragile buried SnO 2 /perovskite interfaces. Conventional interfacial modifiers generally rely on limited coordination, making it difficult to simultaneously achieve robust passivation and strong interfacial adhesion. Here, we introduce two metal-chelating molecules to construct multidentate molecular bridges at the buried interface in C-PSMs. Diglycine disodium (DDS), although capable of coordinating with both SnO 2 and perovskite, exhibits weak configurational selectivity because multiple interfacial coordination modes are energetically accessible, preventing stable bilateral bridge formation. In contrast, tetrasodium iminodisuccinate (IDS) coordinates with both SnO 2 and perovskite, constructing a robust bilateral molecular bridge, suppressing interfacial defects, enhancing charge transfer, and interfacial adhesion. Consequently, the fully printed 30 × 30 cm 2 IDS C-PSMs achieve a record efficiency of 19.26% with an active area of 563.78 cm 2 (certified 18.04%, with a mask-defined area of 487.6 cm 2 ), which represents the highest reported PCE for a device of this size and architecture. The unencapsulated IDS C-PSM retains over 90% of its initial efficiency after 1130 h of maximum-power-point tracking at 65°C and over 85% after 2184 h under damp-heat conditions (85°C and 85% RH).

Scalable Deposition Strategies for All-Inorganic Perovskite Thin Films via Ambient Slot-Die Coating

2026-11-19 · ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces · DOI 10.1021/acsami.6c13543 · [D4*]

Lauriane Scherrer, Emmanuelle Deleporte, Muriel Bouttemy, Karim Medjoubi, Erell Laot, Aimad-Eddine Admane, Jean Rousset, Iwan Zimmermann

Abstract All-inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites, such as CsPbI3, exhibit excellent thermal stability and outstanding optoelectronic properties, making them highly attractive for next-generation photovoltaic and light-emitting applications. Despite these advantages, their practical implementation is hindered by intrinsic polymorphism triggering spontaneous phase transitions toward the non-photoactive yellow δ-phase, severely compromising device performance and long-term stability. To overcome these challenges, we deploy phase-stabilization strategies based on dimethylammonium iodide (DMAI) additive and mixed-halide I/Br compositional engineering to suppress undesired phase formation and enable reproducible, high-quality thin films. Beyond materials design, scalable fabrication remains a critical requirement for industrial adoption. Here, we demonstrate the deposition of CsPbI3-xBrx perovskite films over large areas under ambient atmosphere using slot-die coating. Process optimization combined with effective surface passivation yields devices achieving a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 14.8% for slot-die-coated solar cells fabricated on 5 × 10 cm2 substrates (active area of 0.09 cm2). Furthermore, a five-cell minimodule with an active area of 3.65 cm2 delivers a PCE reaching 13.0%, underscoring the scalability and industrial relevance of the proposed approach. In addition, the device stability, assessed through light cycling tests, reveals promising durability, with devices maintaining 80% of their initial performance (T80) for more than 80 h.

Ionization-State-Directed Sulfonate-Rich Buried Interfaces Enable Durable High-Efficiency Perovskite Solar Cells and Modules

2026-11-06 · Advanced Materials · DOI 10.1002/adma.75660 · [C1 C3 D4 F1* F2]

Yihao Mo, Ye Lan, Hengyu Zhou, Xiaowei Xu, Simeng Gao, Shikang Zhu, Jie Yu, Leixi He, Songyu Du, Quan Liu, Wei Song, Lihong Zhou, Mengjin Yang, Ziyi Ge

ABSTRACT Proton-triggered degradation at the buried interface severely undermines the operational stability of inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs). To address this issue, this work develops an ionization-state-directed sulfonate approach using sodium 2,2'-disulfanediyldiethanesulfonate (DDANA). The pre-deprotonated sulfonate terminals grant DDANA strong coordination with lead sites and drive its spontaneous aggregation at the perovskite and self-assembled monolayer interface, forming robust multisite binding with the perovskite lattice. The enriched sulfonate groups build favorable interfacial dipoles to optimize energy level alignment and facilitate hole transport. The immobilized sulfonate groups act as conjugate-based sites to competitively capture protons, while the disulfide skeleton enables reversible iodine-redox to scavenge corrosive iodine species under operational stress. Dissociated sodium (Na + ) ions embed into lattice interstices and release residual tensile strain. As a result, DDANA-modified inverted PSCs achieve a champion efficiency of 27.38% (certified 27.13%). The strategy extends seamlessly to flexible devices and rigid large-area modules, delivering efficiencies of 25.88% and 23.33%, respectively. Moreover, the DDANA-modified devices retain 98.9% of their initial efficiency after 1000 h of maximum power point tracking and 97.4% after cycling diurnals. This work establishes sulfonate ionization-state engineering as a versatile molecular design platform for constructing chemically robust buried interfaces toward efficient, stable, and scalable perovskite photovoltaics.

Disrupting Solvent Self-Association via Dipole-Dipole Interactions for Improved Performance of Perovskite Solar Cell

2026-10-16 · Advanced Energy Materials · DOI 10.1002/aenm.71715 · [D4 D5 F1*]

Yuqi Du, Wenxiang Xiang, Jiahui Li, Shanwen Wang, Jing Chen, Zhaokui Wang, Geping Qu, Zong‐Xiang Xu, Xiangqian Shen, Liyuan Han, Yanbo Wang

ABSTRACT High-quality perovskite films demand strongly coordinating solvents (SCS, e.g., DMSO, NMP) for crystal growth regulation, yet such solvents often leave residue and induce notorious voids. Prior work elucidated the voids distribution along the vertical direction but cannot explain their lateral dimensions and spatial patterning. Here, we find that the hundreds-of-nanometer lateral dimension and clustered morphology of buried voids originate from the self-association of SCS. The tailored-made 1-bromo-4-dimethylphosphoryl-benzene (BPPO) with inherently weak intermolecular interaction is introduced, which mainly exists in a monomeric form and can efficiently modify the buried interface by constructing dipole-dipole interactions with SCS. The self-association is disrupted, enabling uniform SCS distribution rather than forming big cluster, which accelerates the escape and ultimately eliminates voids at the buried interface. Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) based on BPPO show an efficiency increase from 25.2% to 27.2% (certified efficiency of 27.0%, aperture area of 0.0712 cm 2 ), with improved stability under ISOS-D-2 and ISOS-L-2 protocols. Furthermore, the BPPO-modified perovskite solar module (PSMs, aperture area of 17.4 cm 2 ) achieved an efficiency of 24.2%, demonstrating its excellent scalability.

ABSTRACT Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) offer strong potential for sustainable energy conversion, yet their reliance on toxic-lead (Pb) continues to restrict environmental compatibility and large-scale deployment. Thiourea Derivatives for Mitigating Photodegradation-Mediated Lead Leakage in Sustainable Perovskite Solar Cells and Modules

2026-08-25 · ACS Energy Letters · DOI 10.1021/acsenergylett.6c02033 · [D4 F3*]

Ruotong Kang, Jiakang Zhang, He Sun, Jihao Zhao, Bingqian Zhang, Sirui Han, Wei Tan, Siyu Song, Chenyang Xia, Chengxu Yang, Shuo Jiao, Enqi Zhao, Zhongmin Zhou

Abstract Lead leakage remains a critical barrier for the practical deployment of perovskite solar cells. However, the mechanistic link between device degradation and lead release is still not fully understood. Here, we identify that continuous illumination induces iodide (I-) loss and oxidation to molecular iodine (I2), generating abundant iodine vacancies (VI) within the perovskite lattice. These vacancies destabilize the perovskite octahedral framework, resulting in the exposure of undercoordinated Pb2+ sites at grain boundaries and surfaces. When devices are damaged and subsequently exposed to water, these exposed Pb2+ sites are highly vulnerable to dissolution, forming soluble lead-containing species and serving as the primary source of lead leakage. To address this issue, we develop a dual-regulation strategy based on thiourea-derived molecules targeting both bulk and interfacial regions. Phenylthiourea (TPU) is introduced into the perovskite bulk, where its thiourea moiety coordinates strongly with Pb2+, suppressing defect formation and retarding ion migration. At the device interface, a 3,5-bis(trifluoromethyl)phenylthiourea (TFPU) layer is constructed, where the fluorinated groups enhance hydrophobicity and act as a barrier against moisture ingress, thereby mitigating lead leakage pathways. The optimized device delivers a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 26.13%, providing a mechanistically guided strategy toward high-performance and environmentally more robust perovskite photovoltaics.

Large-Scale Perovskite Photovoltaics: Mitigating Defect Challenges in Upscaled Modules and Monolithic Tandem Cells

2026-08-19 · Advanced Functional Materials · REVIEW · DOI 10.1002/adfm.202532038 · [D4*]

Lvzhou Li, Tong Wang, Xiangli Wen, Kaihuai Du, Chenguang Zhou, Yibo Xu, Xu Dong, Aili Wang, Ningyi Yuan, Jianning Ding

ABSTRACT Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have achieved certified power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding 27% in small-area laboratory devices (typically < 1 cm 2 ), demonstrating considerable potential for commercialization. However, transitioning from small-area devices to upscaled modules invariably results in a significant reduction in both PCE and operational stability. This performance loss primarily originates from the complex crystallization dynamics and the cross-scale coupling effects of defects intrinsic to large-area fabrication processes. This review provides a systematic analysis of the formation mechanisms of defects in large-area perovskite photovoltaic modules and full-size perovskite-silicon tandem cells. It specifically addresses the evolution of multi-level defects, spanning from the atomic scale to the micrometer scale and further to the macroscopic scale. Building upon this mechanistic understanding, the review summarizes cross-dimensional strategies for controlling these multi-scale defects, encompassing compositional engineering, additive engineering, interface modification, and advanced process optimization. Finally, we present a forward-looking perspective on key research directions aimed at overcoming the “efficiency-stability-cost” triangular balance that currently constrains the pathway to widespread industrialization.

Here we discuss the effect of these photo/electrochemical redox reactions, taking into account not only commonly discussed halide oxidation but also irreversible reactions involving organic cations. We summarize possible oxidation and reduction reactions and outline key degradation pathways of organic cations under illumination and bias, highlighting their critical impact on the long-term stability of perovskite solar cells and the existing gaps in understanding. Finally, we discuss approaches to inhibit these undesirable reactions through organic cation design, additive incorporation and device architecture optimization. Oxidation and reduction reactions undermine the stability of perovskite solar cells under illumination and bias. In this Perspective, Ren et al. examine the impact of irreversible reactions involving organic cations, identify key knowledge gaps, and outline strategies for mitigation.

Kilogram-scale one-pot synthesis of multicomponent fullerene composites for efficient inverted perovskite solar cells

2026-03-12 · Nature Communications · DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-70022-0 · [A1 D4 D5 F2*]

Enlong Hou, Shuo Cheng, Song Kong, Yujue Qiu, Jingfu Chen, Xingyu Chen, Shanshan Chen, Yiming Xing, Jinxin Yang, Liqiang Xie, Xinjing Zhao, Tongle Bu, Zhanhua Wei, Chengbo Tian

The high cost and detrimental self-aggregation of conventional single-component fullerenes such as C60 and PCBM constitute a major obstacle to the commercialization of inverted perovskite solar cells (IPSCs). We report a kilogram-scale, one-pot synthesis of a multi-component fullerene composite (FC), comprising C60, a bis((3-methyloxetan-3-yl)methyl) malonate-C60 mono-adduct (BCM), and its bis-adduct (BCB). FC is obtained in 96% yields without complex column chromatography, significantly reducing production costs. Upon thermal annealing, BCM and BCB undergo cross-linking to form a robust encapsulation network that homogeneously incorporates C60, enhancing film stability and electron mobility. IPSCs incorporating cross-linked fullerene composite (CFC) demonstrate an impressive efficiency of 26.55%, surpassing that of PCBM-based devices (24.82%). Additionally, CFC-based devices maintain 96.0% and 95.1% of their initial efficiency after 1000 hours under ISOS-L-1 and ISOS-D-2 protocols, respectively. Notably, CFC demonstrates excellent performance across a range of device configurations, including wide-bandgap (1.68 eV and 1.77 eV) cells, large-area devices (1 cm2), and mini-modules (14.4 cm2). High cost and aggregation of single-component fullerenes hinder inverted perovskite solar cell development. The authors synthesize a fullerene composite in high yield that cross-links during annealing, improving stability and efficiency while maintaining performance in various device formats.
