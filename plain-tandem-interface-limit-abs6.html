Robust Dipicolinic Acid-Anchored Self-Assembled Monolayers for High-Performance Wide-Bandgap and Tandem Perovskite Photovoltaics

2026-10-19 · Advanced Materials · DOI 10.1002/adma.75443 · [B1 F2*]

Yuxiao Guo, Yiren Gao, Daqing Zhang, Xu Li, Chong Zhang, Esmaeil Sheibani, Jinhai Huang, Tingwei He, Lars Kloo, Bo Xu

ABSTRACT The anchoring group of self-assembled monolayer (SAM) hole-transporting materials dictates both substrate binding and interfacial passivation, serving as a key lever for high-performance perovskite photovoltaics. Herein, we report a novel SAM molecular material, denoted by XS21 , engineered with a multidentate dipicolinic acid (DPA) anchoring core to address conventional adhesion and degradation limitations. From an experimental perspective, the DPA anchor is found to promote molecular chemisorption onto the substrate, potentially originating from the dual-action mechanism of pyridine-Ni coordination coupled with dicarboxylate-metal interaction. Furthermore, XS21 imparts both low acid-dissociation constants ( K a ) and superior ultraviolet (UV) photostability. Consequently, wide-bandgap (1.79 eV) perovskite solar cells containing XS21 deliver a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 21.54% with an exceptional open-circuit voltage of 1.372 V. Crucially, integrating XS21 into monolithic all-perovskite tandem solar cells yields a remarkable PCE of 29.62%, alongside an outstanding operational stability (retaining >90% of initial efficiency after 900 h of continuous operation). This work establishes multi-dentate pyridine-carboxylate anchoring as a vital paradigm for next-generation efficient and stable perovskite photovoltaics.

ABSTRACT Widebandgap (WBG) perovskites are critical for highefficiency tandem solar cells but suffer from rapid crystallization, photoinduced halide segregation, and interfacial losses. We introduce methyl 2(4,4,5,5tetramethyl1,3,2dioxaborolan2yl)1Hindole7carboxylate (MHIC) as a multifunctional modulator for 1.68 eV WBG perovskites. Its methoxycarbonyl and boronate groups form Lewis acid-base and hydrogenbonding interactions with the perovskite lattice, effectively passivating defects, reinforcing the framework, and suppressing halide segregation via lattice stabilization. Concurrently, MHIC creates a favorable interfacial dipole that lowers the holeextraction barrier, while retarding nucleation and crystal growth to yield largegrained, lowdisorder films. Integrated into monolithic twoterminal tandems with TOPCon silicon bottom cells, devices reach 32.04% (0.9025 cm 2 ). This work offers a holistic molecularengineering approach that simultaneously tailors crystallization kinetics, electronic structure, and phase stability, providing a viable route to overcome the persistent efficiency-stability tradeoff in WBG perovskite photovoltaics.

ABSTRACT Molecular additives offer a powerful route to control crystallization kinetics and homogenize component distribution in perovskite semiconductors. However, additives that integrate Pb-related coordination and hydrogen-bonding functionalities within a single molecular framework to jointly regulate crystallization evolution and defect chemistry remain largely underexplored. This work provides a molecular design blueprint for high-performance, durable perovskite-based photovoltaics.

ABSTRACT Tandem solar cells offer an approach to overcoming the Shockley-Queisser limit inherent to single-junction solar devices. Perovskite-based triple-junction cells, particularly the perovskite-perovskite-organic (PPO) configuration, offer a balance of high theoretical power conversion efficiency (PCE), processability, and reduced manufacturing complexity. By combining the two perovskite sub-cells with an organic bulk-heterojunction sub-cell, and guided by optical simulations, we developed triple-junction tandem devices with record V OC (3.31 V) and PCE (23.03%) for the PPO configuration. Our work underscores the role of compositional engineering in advancing perovskite-based multi-junction photovoltaics.
